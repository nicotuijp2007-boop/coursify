# Complaints handbook (sample)

## What counts as a complaint

A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction from a client about our service, our advice or an insurer we placed them with, where the client expects a response. It does not matter whether the client uses the word "complaint", or whether they write, call or tell us at the counter.

Questions are not complaints. When a client only asks how something works, answer the question. When the same client says they are unhappy with how it was handled, it becomes a complaint.

## Registering a complaint

Register every complaint in the complaints log on the day you receive it, even if you solve it straight away. Record the date received, the client, the policy, what the client is unhappy about in their own words, and who is handling it.

Send the client a confirmation within two working days. The confirmation names the person handling the complaint and says when the client can expect an answer.

## Answering within the deadline

We answer every complaint within six weeks of receiving it. If we cannot, we tell the client before the six weeks end, explain why, and give a new date.

When the complaint is about an insurer's decision, we help the client put it to the insurer. We stay the client's contact person and follow up until the insurer answers.

## Escalation

If the client is not satisfied with our final answer, our answer tells them they can go to the independent complaints institute within three months. Never discourage a client from escalating.

Complaints about possible fraud, data leaks or threats go to the compliance officer the same day, not through the normal process.

## Learning from complaints

Every quarter, the team lead reviews the complaints log for patterns: the same product, the same insurer, or the same step in our process. Patterns lead to an improvement action with an owner and a date.
